Job Search Safety — Spotting Fake Recruiters And Job Scams

Job hunting puts your CV, your identity documents and sometimes your bank details in front of strangers who email you. That is exactly the setup fraud is built on. The good news: fraudulent hiring is almost always built from the same handful of patterns, and once you know them they are easy to see.

The rule that covers almost everything below: no legitimate employer, recruiter or agency will ever ask you for your OTP, UPI PIN, card PIN, CVV, bank password, email password, or remote access to your device. Not for onboarding, not for "verification", not for "equipment". Anyone who does is running a scam, and the request itself proves it.

1. The Six Standard Job Scams

ScamHow it worksEarly warning
Advance-fee fraudYou are asked to pay a registration, training, security, visa or "processing" fee to be considered or hired.Any candidate payment at all. Employers charge employers.
Fake recruitment / impersonationSomeone uses a real company's name and a very similar email domain to offer you a high salary you did not apply for.Unsolicited high offer, company domain is one letter off, no listing on their careers page.
Cheque or overpayment scam"Equipment" is sent to you by courier; you are told to forward part of it and return the rest.You are asked to move money on someone else's behalf.
Fake check / money-mulePayments land in your account for a "job", with instructions to pass funds on to others.Your pay arrives before you have started work.
Identity theftA genuine-looking application harvests your Aadhaar, PAN, passport, bank details and photos.They ask for identity documents before any conversation.
Task / money-making scamSimple tasks (rating products, boosting orders) that pay a little, then require a deposit to "unlock" bigger tasks.Earn money by rating or clicking; paid to join; escalating deposits.
Tricked into paying a "scam"They insist you buy gift cards or crypto to pay for a job, interview, or your own background check.Unusual payment method; receipt required; urgency.
Malware link or "interview app"A link to a fake interview portal, or you are asked to install software that gives them screen access.Any app or remote-access tool at any stage of hiring.

2. The Verification Routine — Do It Every Time

Never verify by replying to the message you were suspicious of. Verify by going to the source yourself.

  1. Find the company's official website independently. Type the name yourself, or use a search engine. Do not click the link in the message.
  2. Check their careers page. Does your role exist there? A real opening is almost always listed, even for agencies advertising on the employer's behalf.
  3. Read the email domain carefully. "acmecorp-jobs.com" is not "acmecorp.com". Check for added hyphens, spelled-out words, extra letters, or a country-code ending.
  4. Look for the recruiter elsewhere. A real recruiter has a real history. Search their name plus the company on LinkedIn or the company's own site.
  5. Call the company's official number yourself. Ask whether the recruiter is real and whether the role is open. This is the single most reliable test, and it takes two minutes.
  6. Verify before you hand anything over. Nothing is sent until the company and the person are both confirmed.
Verify the company, not just the domain. It is possible for a scammer to control a real-looking email domain briefly, or to use a free service to receive mail. Confirming through a phone number you looked up yourself closes that gap.

3. Safe Job Searching Habits

  • Keep one master CV and create a trimmed copy per application — so a single scam cannot harvest everything at once.
  • Use a dedicated email address for job searching, separate from your personal one. You can delete it after.
  • Remove date of birth, marital status, nationality and full address from the CV. No employer in a professional context needs them, and their absence costs you nothing.
  • Do not upload your identity documents early. Legitimate onboarding happens after an offer, on a secure portal you reached yourself.
  • Never do a video interview on an unknown app from a link in a message. Know the platform before you accept.
  • Be suspicious of anyone who wants your device. Install nothing for a job interview. Ever.
  • Set alerts on your job portal and company career pages, so you know when a real role actually opens.

4. If You Have Already Responded

Do not wait to see what happens next. Work through this in order.

  1. Stopped anything already running. If you installed an app, granted remote access or shared your screen — disconnect, revoke access, and change your email password on a clean device.
  2. Changed your email password first. Email is the reset route for every other account, so protect it before anything else.
  3. Changed other passwords that share a password with your email, and turned on two-factor authentication.
  4. Checked your bank account and reported anything unfamiliar to your bank's fraud helpline on the number printed on your card.
  5. Reported the fraud. Report to the cybercrime portal or local police with screenshots, and get a reference number. For a financial fraud you need a police complaint, not just a bank call.
  6. Watch for the second wave. Recovery services will contact you claiming they can retrieve your money, for a fee. This is almost always another scam. See the home page alert for why that pattern works.
  7. If your documents were sent, inform your bank, your identity-document issuer, and monitor for identity misuse.

5. Common Questions About Job Scams

SituationVerdict
"Pay £40 for a mandatory background check"Fraud. Candidates are never charged for screening.
"We will send you a laptop; deposit the amount as security"Overpayment scam. Refuse.
"Send your bank details so we can pay your first salary by transfer"Your salary is not paid into your account before you work. Refuse.
"Install this app and share your screen for the assessment"Malware risk. Assessments do not need remote access.
"Sign in with your OTP to verify your identity on our portal"Fraud. This is OTP theft.
"We have no careers page, but the role is real"Possible for genuine agencies, but unverifiable. Insist on verification before sharing anything.
"We will reimburse your travel for the interview"Reasonable, but do not accept cash or a card payment request, and check the offer independently first.

6. Protecting Others

If you spot a scam, report it. Most job boards have a "report" button on the listing; the impersonated company's abuse address is usually on its website. Report to the cybercrime authority with screenshots and the exact wording. It costs you ten minutes, and it is often the only way another applicant finds out in time.

Speed kills these scams. A confident scammer depends on you feeling rushed and flattered. Thirty seconds of verification is enough to break almost every attempt. If a recruiter is real, they will not mind you taking a day to check.
