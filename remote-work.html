Remote Work Basics — Working Well From Home

Remote work rewards people who are deliberate about structure, communication and boundaries. It punishes people who wait to be told what to do next. This guide covers setting up, working visibly without being online all day, running asynchronous communication, securing your data, and staying connected to a team you rarely see.

The core shift: in an office your presence is visible. Remote, it is not. Everything that used to happen by overhearing — the decision made in the corridor, the deadline that moved, the person who needs help — now has to be written down. That single change explains most remote-work advice you will ever read.

1. Setting Up: What Actually Matters

2. Working Visible Without Being Online All Day

You do not need to be present to be visible. You need to be predictable.

  • Keep agreed hours and honour them. The clearest signal to a remote manager is that you are reachable when you say you will be.
  • Write things down. Decisions, blockers and status belong in shared channels where others can find them, not in your head.
  • Post a short daily or weekly update. Three lines: what you did, what is next, what is blocking you. This removes the need to chase you entirely.
  • Say when you are stuck early. An hour of silence looks like indifference; a question in the morning looks like competence.
  • Finish visibly. Close tasks with a message describing the outcome, not just "done".
The mistake remote workers make most: staying silent all day and expecting credit for the work. Output is invisible by default. Say what you did, in writing, to the people who decide things.

3. Communication Norms That Prevent Friction

Most remote conflict is not personal — it is a missing expectation. Agree these explicitly, in writing, with your team.

QuestionAgree on
What is urgent?Define it: production down, customer escalation, a deadline within 24 hours. Everything else is not urgent, however much it feels like it.
Which channel for what?Chat for quick coordination, email for anything needing a record, calls for real discussion, ticketing system for tracked work.
Response timesReasonable expectations per channel. Nobody reads chat instantly and that is fine.
Meeting policyWho must attend, who may skip, agenda in advance, decision recorded afterwards.
Core hoursA shared window where everyone is reachable, so you are not permanently on call.

4. Async Communication That Actually Works

  1. Lead with the ask. First line says what you need and by when. "Can you review this by Thursday?" beats a paragraph of context first.
  2. Give context before detail. Say why you are asking, then what you did, then what you need.
  3. One topic per message. A message with three asks gets two answered.
  4. Use threads properly. Reply in the thread so others can follow; do not restart the conversation in a new chat.
  5. Say when you are done. "@mention closing this, nothing further needed" prevents the follow-up nobody wanted.
  6. Document decisions where search finds them. Not in your personal notes.

5. Security & Privacy At Home

  • Lock the screen whenever you step away, even at home. Family and visitors do not care about your confidentiality.
  • Use a separate work device profile or machine where policy requires it, and never share work accounts or MFA authenticators with anyone.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication on email, company tools and cloud storage. Use an authenticator app, not SMS where offered.
  • Check your home network — a current router with WPA2 or WPA3 and a changed default password.
  • Use a company VPN when required, and never access company systems from public Wi-Fi without it.
  • Be careful with shared links — set access to "specific people" rather than "anyone with the link" on anything sensitive.
  • Clear your local downloads of confidential files when you no longer need them.
Your employer will never ask you for your OTP or MFA code — not by chat, not by email, not by a "IT support" call. Anyone who does is trying to log in as you. See job search safety for the same scams on the hiring side.

6. Boundaries: The Actual Hard Part

Remote work collapses the walls between work and home, so you have to build them deliberately.

  • Fixed start and finish. Close the laptop at a set time. It defines your day far more than any task list.
  • Separate spaces where possible. If work happens at the dining table, stop working at the dining table.
  • Mute the work chat after hours. You are not on call because the app is open.
  • Tell your household. Interruptions at home are the most common complaint; a visible "in a meeting until 3" sign works.
  • Take real breaks. Lunch away from the screen. Standing every hour. It is not a productivity trick, it is basic maintenance.

7. Connection & Progression

The career risk of going remote is visibility, not competence. Counter it deliberately.

  • Make your work findable — share outcomes in the channel your manager reads, not just in your own tasks.
  • Book one-to-ones with your manager and your skip-level. Keep the same rhythm as in an office.
  • Ask for feedback in a specific area rather than "any feedback", or you will get "all good".
  • Stay connected to colleagues you would have met by accident in an office — see workplace communication and networking.
  • Go into the office occasionally and deliberately. Turn up to people, not just to a desk.
