Frequently Asked Questions

The questions people actually ask about resumes, interviews, job scams, remote work and getting ahead. If yours is not here, send it to us — and if it is a good one, we will add it.

About this site

Is this site free?

Completely. No sign-up, no paywall, no email required to read anything. There is no paid tier and no “premium” unlock, because paywalling career advice mostly locks out the people who need it most.

Do you help me find a job?

No. We are not recruiters and we do not have a job board, an agency, or a talent database. We publish general guidance that helps you run your own search, and we do not charge fees on anyone's behalf. If a site offers to submit your CV “for a fee”, that is a red flag in itself.

Is this legal, HR or financial advice?

No. This is general educational information. For anything involving employment contracts, labour law, taxes, visa status or pay disputes, talk to a qualified professional who knows your situation and your country — rules differ substantially between jurisdictions.

Do you store my data or sell it?

There are no accounts, so no profile exists. We do not sell or share personal data, we do not run advertising trackers, and the only information we hold is what you choose to send in an enquiry plus basic server logs kept for security. See the privacy policy.

Resumes

How long should my resume be?

One page for under ten years of experience, two pages for ten or more. Senior, research and academic roles often justify two. Content and results matter more than page count — but a dense wall of text gets skimmed and dropped, so cutting is usually an improvement.

Should I use a resume template?

Only if it is a simple single-column layout. Fancy two-column designs, text boxes, icons and charts break the parsers that applicant tracking systems use to read your file. Anything the parser cannot read is invisible to the recruiter. See the resume guide.

Do I need a cover letter?

For most roles, no — the resume does the work. If a posting asks for one, write it fresh but short: why this role, what in your history proves you can do it, and one specific reason for this company. A generic cover letter is worse than none, because it signals you mass-applied.

I have a career gap. How do I explain it?

Explain it once, briefly, in the summary, without defensiveness: what you did, what you learned, why you are ready now. If you did anything useful during the gap — caring for a family member, studying, freelancing, health recovery — describe it in those terms. Then move straight to what you have done since.

Interviews

How do I answer “tell me about yourself”?

Present to relevant, then past, then why this role. Around ninety seconds, ending on why you want this job specifically. It is a positioning answer, not a biography — and it is the easiest question to prepare and the most commonly wasted.

Should I send a thank-you email?

Yes, within 24 hours. Keep it under 120 words, name something specific from the conversation, and briefly restate why you want the role. It is a small signal that most candidates skip.

Job search safety

Will a real employer ever ask for my OTP or bank details?

Never. No employer, recruiter or agency needs your OTP, UPI PIN, card PIN, CVV, bank password or email password for any hiring purpose. Any such request is a scam, and the request itself is the proof.

How do I know a recruiter is legitimate?

Find the company on its own official website and careers page, confirm the role is listed there, check whether the recruiter has a real presence connected to that company, then call the company's published number yourself to confirm. Verify through a channel you chose — never through the link you were sent.

I already replied to a suspicious message. What now?

Stop anything already running. If you installed an app or shared your screen, disconnect and revoke access. Change your email password first, then any password that shared it, and turn on two-factor authentication. Check your bank and report to your bank's fraud line, then file a police report for any financial loss. Watch for a second wave of “recovery” services. Full checklist in the safety guide.

Remote work

How do I stay productive working from home?

Use time-blocking, a fixed start and end time, and a separate workspace if you can manage it. Keep a written list of the day's three priorities, batch messaging into a few check-in windows, and take real breaks away from the desk. Most remote productivity problems are actually boundary problems.

How do I prove I am working when I am remote?

Do not try to prove it by being constantly online. Be visible through predictable communication, reliable delivery, clear status updates and good documentation. What you are measured on is output and trust, and both come from predictability rather than presence.

Work & progression

How do I give feedback to a colleague?

Be specific about the behaviour and its effect, not about the person. Describe what you observed, explain the impact, then suggest what would be better. Do it soon after the event, and ask a question rather than delivering a verdict.

How do I get promoted?

By doing the next role well before you are given it, and making it visible in writing. Most managers promote on evidence, not potential. Ask what the level above requires, take on scope that is genuinely harder, deliver it reliably, and record the results. See career growth.

How do I negotiate salary without seeming difficult?

Give the reason for your number before you give the number. Researched market data for the role and location, a competing offer, or a specific qualification they were asking for are all legitimate reasons. Then ask them to close the gap — and do it once, to one person, not four times to four people.
